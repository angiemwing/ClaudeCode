01 — Project Foundation

What if course data had a brand system?

This project started with a question: what if the three healthcare datasets from my Data Analytics course belonged to one fictional organization — and that organization had a visual identity that carried through every chart, dashboard, and presentation?

Hale Health is a multi-state regional hospital system across Arizona, New Mexico, Utah, and Colorado. Four service lines, ~100K patient encounters annually, ~1,700 staff. Built through acquisition, operating under separate legacy identities — until now.

03 — Market Context

The Four Corners: one of the most underserved regions in the country.

Rural, culturally diverse — Native American, Hispanic, Anglo — with diabetes prevalence exceeding 20% among Native American adults. 30 physicians per 100K in rural areas versus 263 urban. 96% of New Mexico's rural counties face primary care shortages.

The brand had to communicate stability and rootedness. And the data visualizations informing leadership decisions had to look like they belonged to that same identity.

04 — Competitive Analysis

Blues, greens, whites. Stock photography. Generic messaging.

San Juan Regional, CommonSpirit/Mercy Durango, Banner Health, IHS/Navajo Area — every competitor defaults to the same clinical palette and safe visual language. Nobody owns the intersection of warmth, clinical depth, and cultural specificity.

That's the gap Hale Health fills. Grounded in place, modern in execution, built for the long haul.

09 — Naming

Hale: healthy, whole, sound.

From Old English. It sounds like "heal" without being literal. Against the brown and blue palette, it reads as sturdy and approachable — a name that belongs on a building you'd trust.

Branded house model: Hale Health — Farmington, Hale Health — Durango. One system, one name, one identity across four states.

10 — How the Brand Extends to Data

This is the core of the project.

The brand isn't just a logo — it's a system that governs how data is presented and how narratives are told.

Data visualizations — every chart uses Hale Health's color system, typography, and layout standards. Attrition, patient outcomes, length of stay, regression analyses — all branded consistently.

Data narratives — the brand voice carries into findings. Direct, human, jargon-free. Data tells a story about real people, not abstract metrics.

Presentation storytelling — a deck for the CMO about resource utilization looks like it came from the same organization as a patient-facing brochure.

11 — Strategy Brief

The brief in three lines.

Design a brand that feels like the earth it's built on — warm, steady, honest. Espresso, Terracotta, Blush, and Sky. Lora and Inter. Direct and human.

Then extend that brand into every data visualization, dashboard, and presentation the organization produces — proving that analytics and design are not separate disciplines.
